For delegates · Working draft Background for your deliberation. This describes how decisions are made in Utah now. It does not recommend what should change — that is your work.

Where can the public influence AI decisions in Utah today?

You have been asked what role the public should have in shaping decisions about AI technology and infrastructure. Before deciding what that role should be, it helps to know what it is. This page sets out which bodies make these decisions, what say the public currently has before each one, and when those opportunities happen.

00

Schools and classrooms

The law requires a public meeting. A body must hold a meeting and take comment before it decides.

Verified

The question this raisesWhose meeting is the decision made in, and who gets to speak at it?

What gets decided, and by whomEvery Utah school district must adopt a policy on AI use in its schools. Before adopting it, the district's governing board must hold a public meeting that allows public comment, and must then confirm to the state that the meeting happened. Around ten districts across Cache, Salt Lake and Utah Counties will each do this between December 2026 and June 2027.

Electricity costs

You can take part, but as a formal party. There is a process, and it is open — but it is built for organisations with lawyers.

Verified

The question this raisesIs someone paying an electricity bill a party to a case about that bill, or an audience for it?

What gets decided, and by whomThe Public Service Commission decides whether the power company can serve a large new customer, what new equipment that needs, and who pays for it. Utilities, large customers and state offices file evidence as parties. Members of the public can speak at a public witness hearing. Those are not the same thing.

Where data centres get built

You can comment if the body allows it. Meetings are open and comment is usually taken, but nothing requires the body to seek it or answer it.

Verified

The question this raisesAt what point does the public get a right to be heard — and can that right be used up by a decision made before anyone knew a project existed?

What gets decided, and by whomNormally a county or city decides what can be built where, and a rezoning carries a public hearing. But some state entities can take land into a 'project area' and then act as the land use authority themselves. Where that happens, the county's agreement is given once and cannot be withdrawn.

Water use

You can comment if the body allows it. Meetings are open and comment is usually taken, but nothing requires the body to seek it or answer it.

Verified

The question this raisesWhen a public body learns a project will use a large amount of water, does the public learn it at the same time?

What gets decided, and by whomSince July 2026, before approving a large data centre a local authority must notify the state water agencies and the water provider, and operators must report their expected water use. The notice goes to three government bodies. Residents are not among them.

Tax breaks

You can comment if the body allows it. Meetings are open and comment is usually taken, but nothing requires the body to seek it or answer it.

Verified

The question this raisesCan the public be heard about a figure nobody has worked out?

What gets decided, and by whomUtah exempts qualifying data centres from some sales tax. Because the exemption is applied at the till, the state does not collect figures on how much revenue it gives up each year. The State Auditor has confirmed the information is not readily available and is building a public dashboard.

How the state sets AI policy

You find out after it is decided. The decision is published once made. There is no notice beforehand and no comment period.

Verified

The question this raisesWhen the state agrees to set aside a rule for a particular company, does anyone outside the room find out before it is signed?

What gets decided, and by whomUtah's Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy can agree with a company to relax specific Utah regulations for twelve months while a product is tested. These agreements are published once signed. Nothing requires notice or public comment before that.

AI at work

No say in the decision — only a complaint afterwards. You have no standing while the decision is made. There is a route to object once it is done, if you can find out enough to use it.

Verified

The question this raisesAre you entitled to know that a machine was involved in a decision about your job?

What gets decided, and by whomUtah has passed no law about employers using AI to hire, monitor, evaluate, discipline or dismiss workers. Nothing requires an employer to tell you AI was used, to show how it reached its conclusion, or to test the tool for bias. If the outcome discriminates on a protected basis — race, sex, age, disability, religion and others — that is already unlawful, and you can file with the Utah Labor Commission's Antidiscrimination and Labor Division or the federal EEOC within 180 days, if your employer has 15 or more employees. The protection covers the result. Nothing covers the process, which is how you would learn there was anything to complain about. For comparison: a school using generative AI with your child must notify you in writing.

02

What these terms mean

Words that appear above, and that will come up again over the two days.

Public comment
A chance to speak or write to a body before it decides. Often time-limited, and the body is not required to answer you.
Formal party
A step beyond comment. A party files evidence, questions the other side, and gets a written response. Usually requires a lawyer or an organisation.
Land use authority
The body that decides what can be built where. Usually a county or city, but in Utah some state entities hold it too.
Interim committee
Legislators meet between sessions to study subjects and prepare bills. Utah's session is short, so much of the real work happens here.
Rulemaking
When an agency writes the detailed rules a law leaves to it. Rules are published for a comment period before taking effect.
Initiative & referendum
Citizen petitions. An initiative proposes a new law; a referendum asks voters to overturn one. Both need signatures, and Utah's thresholds are high.

Who has agreed to respond

A receiver is a body that agrees in advance to take up your recommendations on the record, by a set date. Agreeing to respond is not agreeing to say yes. A carrier is a different thing: an organisation that campaigns for a recommendation after you go home. A body cannot carry a petition to itself, so receivers and carriers are always different people.

This section will list the bodies that have committed to respond, and by when. Ask your organisers where that stands — it is a fair question, and the answer shapes what your recommendations are worth.

05

Where no one currently holds the question

These are not complaints about decisions that went the wrong way. They are places where the law provides no way for the public to be heard at all, because no such route was ever created.

Land taken into a state project area

A state entity can take land into a project area and become the land use authority for it. No public hearing is required before that happens, and the county's agreement cannot be withdrawn afterwards or put to voters.

Knowing AI was used at work

If an employer's AI tool discriminates on a protected basis, that is already unlawful, and the Utah Labor Commission and the EEOC both take complaints. What is missing is earlier: nothing requires an employer to tell you AI was used, to explain how it reached its conclusion, or to check the tool for bias. The remedy exists. Reaching it depends on finding out something nobody has to tell you.

The state's model school AI policy

Before adopting core school standards, the state board must publish a draft, take 90 days of comment, and hold three public hearings. For the model AI policy, none of that applies. The public meeting requirement falls on districts, not on the state.

Agreements setting aside regulations

Nothing requires notice or comment before the state agrees to relax a rule for a named company. The agreement is published after it is signed.

Notice of large water commitments

Before a large data centre is approved, three government bodies must be told. Residents of the affected area are not among them.

The cost of the tax exemption

No figure exists for what the data centre tax exemption costs each year. A hearing cannot be held about a number nobody has.

A commitment with no method

A May 2026 executive order tells state agencies to carry out extensive public engagement on data centres. It names no agency, no venue, no timeline and nothing they must produce.
