Why now

As AI reaches deeper into our economy and public life, decisions are being made about our communities largely without us. Right now almost no community has a legitimate, cross-partisan way to weigh in on where there is super majority agreement across the political divides — and to turn that agreement into change.

Data centers site next to neighborhoods.
Districts adopt classroom AI overnight.
Public dollars underwrite private infrastructure.
Residents are scored by systems they never consented to.

Packed hearings and overflowing comment periods show residents want a real say. Most communities simply have no legitimate way to give them one.

Why this matters

Beyond polls and petitions.

A poll tells you what people think. A petition tells you they're upset. Neither builds what your community actually needs: a coalition rooted in common ground that can turn agreement into real policy change — and keep doing it.

A coalition, not a moment

The cross-partisan steering committee and the relationships it builds outlast the assembly — a standing table that keeps meeting, and keeps mending trust across political lines, long after the report is filed.

Real policy impact

Supermajority findings carried directly into councils, districts, agencies, and legislatures — action on the issues that actually shape your community, not a study that sits on a shelf.

Civic agency, built to last

Your own people gain the skills and standing to run this again on the next hard question — a community that has practiced governing together, not just being asked its opinion.

This goes beyond polls and petitions — it's collaborative self-governance: a community that decides together, acts together, and gets stronger for having done it.

How it works

One arc, run by you, supported by us.

Every host runs the same five-part spine, adapted to local ground. You convene and carry; BLOOM trains, resources, and connects.

01

Commission

A cross-partisan steering committee engages and listens to discern the right question — including a political landscape assessment — then commits, in advance, to act on the answer.

02

Engage

Broad public conversations, online and in person, help residents make sense of the issue together — surfacing common ground and the real experience underneath it.

03

Deliberate

A Civic Assembly — residents chosen by lottery, demographically representative of your region — works through expert input and trade-offs to a considered answer.

04

Carry

Recommendations reaching supermajority agreement are advanced with local institutions, city and county officials, school boards, and state representatives where state policy is implicated.

05

Federate

Findings from all six regions combine into a mandate with weight at larger scales — state, national, and direct to private and civil society actors.

The toolkit

Technology and practices for listening at scale.

CivicOS is our own civic technology stack, co-designed to your context. It is open source and free to use — yours to keep, adapt, and run long after the cohort ends. It does the work of hearing a whole community, so your team can focus on organizing it.

OpenPoll

Map the opinion landscape of your whole region. Residents share their own views and respond to their neighbors', and the analysis reveals where common ground already exists to build from — along with the questions still genuinely open, which are exactly the ones deliberation can unlock.

Live conversation capture

Transcription tools for community meetings, in person and online, so what people actually say in the room becomes part of the record instead of evaporating. Small-group conversations at scale, captured and synthesized.

Reporting & analysis

Clear reporting for your coalition and for the public — showing where agreement is strong, where it divides, and what that means for the path forward. Shared evidence everyone can navigate by.

Who we are

Tested practice, not a pilot on you.

This method has been run more than a thousand times around the world, and the pattern is consistent enough to plan around. We are bringing tested practice to your community, along with the tools and training to run it yourselves.

BLOOM is a team of civic practitioners, technologists, and organizers. Our method builds on the Civic Jury tradition of The Jefferson Center, the large-scale citizen deliberations of AmericaSpeaks, and open-source listening technology, reviewed by leaders in the field including Audrey Tang. Led by Rahmin Sarabi and Clara Long, with prior work spanning the Fort Collins Civic Assembly and Engaged California. We operate under the Association for Civic Infrastructure.
