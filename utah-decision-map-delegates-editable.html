Where can the public influence AI decisions in Utah today?

You have been asked what role the public should have in shaping decisions about AI technology and infrastructure. Before deciding what that role should be, it helps to know what it is. This page sets out which bodies make these decisions, what say the public currently has before each one, and when those opportunities happen.

00

How to read this

Every claim here was checked against a source. Some are firmer than others, and you should know which is which.

Verified

Checked against a named source — a statute, an agency page, meeting minutes, a county record — with a date. You can rely on this.

Moving

True as of the date given, but the situation is changing. Worth asking whether it still holds.

Inferred

Worked out from related facts rather than found in a source. Treat as a question, not an answer.

Public comment
A chance to speak or write to a body before it decides. Often time-limited, and the body is not required to answer you.
Formal party
A step beyond comment. A party files evidence, questions the other side, and gets a written response. Usually requires a lawyer or an organisation.
Land use authority
The body that decides what can be built where. Usually a county or city, but in Utah some state entities hold it too.
Interim committee
Legislators meet between sessions to study subjects and prepare bills. Utah's session is short, so much of the real work happens here.
Rulemaking
When an agency writes the detailed rules a law leaves to it. Rules are published for a comment period before taking effect.
Initiative & referendum
Citizen petitions. An initiative proposes a new law; a referendum asks voters to overturn one. Both need signatures, and Utah's thresholds are high.

Who has agreed to respond

A receiver is a body that agrees in advance to take up your recommendations on the record, by a set date. Agreeing to respond is not agreeing to say yes. A carrier is a different thing: an organisation that campaigns for a recommendation after you go home. A body cannot carry a petition to itself, so receivers and carriers are always different people.

This section will list the bodies that have committed to respond, and by when. Ask your organisers where that stands — it is a fair question, and the answer shapes what your recommendations are worth.
